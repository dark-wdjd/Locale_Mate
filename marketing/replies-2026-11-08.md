### 1. "Chengdu with elderly parent" — reply to the unanswered wheelchair question ⭐ do this first
🔗 https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowTopic-g297463-i8702-k15589772-Chengdu_with_elderly_parent-Chengdu_Sichuan.html
**Context:** She's travelling with her elderly mother, who can walk but slowly. She asked where to rent a wheelchair; the only answer was "most tourist centers provide wheelchairs".

> Adding to the good advice above on the wheelchair question:
>
> - **Ask your hotel first.** Email them before you arrive. Many of the larger Chengdu hotels can lend one or arrange it for you, and the concierge can also write your plans in Chinese for the driver.
> - **At the sights, ask at the visitor centre.** As Frank mentioned, the Dujiangyan visitor hall has wheelchairs and shuttle carts. At other big sites it's worth asking at the entrance or service desk.
> - **Tell your private driver in advance** that you're bringing a wheelchair, so they send a car with enough boot space.
>
> One more tip for the Panda Base if you add it: go for the morning session, when it's cooler and the pandas are most active, and use the sightseeing bus between areas. It saves a lot of walking on the hilly paths.
>
> Have a lovely trip with your mum!

### 2. "Dujiangyan Panda Valley to Chengdu Central" — answer Nabila's question (Oct 11, unanswered)
🔗 https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowTopic-g297463-i8702-k15513307-Dujiangyan_Panda_Valley_to_Chengdu_Central-Chengdu_Sichuan.html
**Context:** Nabila asked in Malay: "Is there transport from the Chengdu Panda Base to Dujiangyan?"

> Hi Nabila — there's no direct public bus from the Chengdu Panda Base to Dujiangyan. They're on opposite sides of Chengdu (the Panda Base is in the north-east, Dujiangyan is about 60 km north-west).
>
> The easiest options:
> 1. **Taxi or DiDi straight there.** Roughly 1–1.5 hours depending on traffic. This is the simplest option if you're two or more people.
> 2. **Metro + intercity train.** Take the metro to Xipu station, then the Chengdu–Dujiangyan intercity train (about 30 minutes). Book the train ahead on Trip.com, and take a taxi from Dujiangyan station to the scenic area or Panda Valley.
>
> If you're doing both on the same day, go to the Panda Base first thing in the morning (the pandas are most active early) and head to Dujiangyan after lunch.

⚠️ **Before posting, check:** that the Chengdu→Dujiangyan intercity train really leaves from Xipu (a 1-minute search on Trip.com trains: "Xipu to Dujiangyan").

### 3. "Gratuity for guide" — optional, lower priority (14 replies, but nobody answered the actual question)
🔗 https://www.tripadvisor.com/ShowTopic-g297462-i8701-k15598708-Gratuity_for_guide-Sichuan.html
**Context:** A 9–10 day private tour with one local guide. They asked for the going rate per day in RMB. Replies argue about whether China has a tipping culture; none gave a practical approach.

> You're hearing both views because both are true: Chinese clients generally don't tip, but on private tours with foreign visitors, many guides do receive a tip at the end and appreciate it. It's a thank-you, not an obligation.
>
> For a 9–10 day private tour, rather than working out a per-day rate, a common approach is:
> - decide at the end, based on how the trip went;
> - give it on the last day in an envelope (a red envelope, 红包, is a nice touch), **separately to the guide and the driver**, since they're often paid separately;
> - if you booked through an agency, ask them what's customary. They'll know what their guides normally receive and won't be offended by the question.
>
> A sincere thank-you note or a good online review also means a lot to independent guides, since that's how they get their next clients.
