## The short answer

The best months to visit the Chengdu Panda Base are **October and November**, followed by **March and April**. The weather is cool enough for the pandas to stay outdoors and active through the morning, and in autumn you may see the year's new cubs. **July and August** are the hardest months: hot, humid and busy, with many pandas moved indoors before mid-morning.

Whatever the month, the time of day matters even more. Go at opening time, when the pandas are eating and most active. Our [Chengdu Panda Base guide](/blog/chengdu-panda-base-guide) covers opening hours, booking with your passport and getting there.

## Why the season matters

Giant pandas come from cool, damp mountain forests, and their thick, oily coat is built for cold weather. They cope with cold much better than heat:

- **In heat, they go indoors.** When it gets hot, keepers move the pandas into air-conditioned indoor rooms. One visitor guide puts the threshold at about **26°C**, which in July and August is often reached by 10:00–10:30. After that, outdoor enclosures can be empty.
- **In cold weather, they stay active.** Pandas don't hibernate, and the Chengdu Panda Breeding Research Foundation notes they are very active in winter, eating, climbing and playing outdoors.

So a cool morning is the best combination you can get.

## Month by month

| Months | Weather in Chengdu | Pandas | Crowds | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **January–February** | Cold and damp (around 6°C on average in January) | Active outdoors | Quiet, except around Spring Festival | Good, if you avoid the holiday |
| **March–April** | Mild and pleasant | Active; spring is mating season | Moderate | Very good |
| **May–June** | Warming up; rainy season starts in June | Active early, indoors sooner as it warms | Busy around 1–5 May | Fine with an early start |
| **July–August** | Hot and humid (about 23–31°C), the wettest months | Often indoors by mid-morning | Busiest: school holidays | Hardest time to visit |
| **September** | Still warm; rainy season ends | More active as it cools; first cubs may appear late in the month | Busy at weekends | Good |
| **October** | Mild (around 18°C on average), drier | Active through the morning; cubs on show | Very busy 1–7 October | Best, after Golden Week |
| **November** | Cool (around 13°C on average) | Very active | Quietest | Best |
| **December** | Cold | Active; cubs still small | Quiet | Very good |

Weather figures are long-term averages; check the forecast for your dates.

## When can you see panda cubs?

Most of the Panda Base's cubs are born between **July and September**, many of them in late July and August. Newborns are tiny, pink and kept with their mothers in the nursery houses (the Sunshine, Moonlight and Star nurseries), where some visitors see them through glass in late summer.

Visitor guides put the cubs' **public debut around late September or early October**, and the best window for seeing them as small, fluffy, rolling youngsters is roughly **October to December**, when they're two to five months old.

A caveat: whether any cubs are on view depends on that year's births and on the base's daily arrangements. Some days the nurseries have nothing on show, so treat cubs as a bonus rather than a promise.

## Dates to avoid

The Panda Base is one of China's most popular attractions with domestic visitors too. These are its busiest periods:

- **National Day Golden Week: 1–7 October.** Tickets sell out and queues are long. If you're visiting in October, come after the 8th.
- **Spring Festival (Chinese New Year).** In 2027 the public holiday runs from **5 to 12 February**.
- **Summer school holidays: July and August.** Combined with the heat, this is the toughest time to visit.
- **Labour Day: 1–5 May.** Busy, though less than Golden Week.

Weekends are busier than weekdays all year round. If you only have one weekday in Chengdu, use it for the pandas.

## The best time of day (in any season)

- **Book the morning session** and be at the gate shortly before it opens: 7:30 from March to October, 8:00 from November to February. Opening times can change, so check your booking.
- **Head for the outdoor enclosures first.** Pandas eat in the early morning and are usually asleep by late morning, often high in a tree.
- **In summer, see the outdoor enclosures first.** By mid-morning many pandas will have moved indoors, so save the indoor houses for later.
- **Rain isn't a problem.** Pandas don't mind it, and a drizzle often thins the crowds.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Visiting in October–November or March–April if your dates are flexible.
- [ ] Avoiding 1–7 October, Spring Festival and, if you can, July–August.
- [ ] A weekday, morning-session ticket booked with your passport.
- [ ] At the gate before opening time.
