## The short answer

Yes, winter is a good time to visit Chengdu, as long as you know what to expect. The city is **cool, damp and often grey** from November to February, and buildings feel colder inside than you might be used to. In return you get **fewer crowds, active pandas, golden ginkgo trees, early plum blossoms and the best hot pot weather of the year**.

The one period to plan around is **Spring Festival (Chinese New Year)**, when travel across China peaks. In 2027 the public holiday runs from **5 to 12 February**.

## Winter weather, month by month

| Month | Typical temperatures | What it's like |
|---|---|---|
| **November** | Highs around 16°C, lows around 9°C | Cool and fairly dry, often pleasant. Ginkgo trees turn gold late in the month |
| **December** | About 5–10°C | Cold and damp, with morning mist. Ginkgo at its best in early to mid-December |
| **January** | About 2–8°C, occasionally near freezing | The coldest month. Grey skies, little rain |
| **February** | About 4–9°C | Slowly warming. Plum blossoms and Spring Festival |

Snow in the city itself is rare. Expect **mist and low cloud**, especially in the mornings, and some days of haze. Rain is light compared with summer.

## The indoor cold: what to know

This surprises many visitors. Chengdu lies south of the line where China switches on central heating in winter, so most homes, small restaurants, teahouses and older buildings are **not centrally heated**. Indoor temperatures of 8–12°C are common, and locals often keep their coats on inside.

What this means for you:

- **Hotels** usually heat rooms with the air-conditioning unit. It works, but it can be dry and slow; check recent reviews if warmth matters to you.
- **Dress in layers you can keep on indoors.** A warm base layer makes a bigger difference than a heavy coat.
- **Hot pot restaurants and malls are warm.** That's part of why hot pot is so popular in winter.

## What's good about Chengdu in winter

### Active pandas and quieter mornings

Giant pandas are built for cold weather and stay active outdoors in winter, rather than retreating indoors as they do on hot summer days. Winter mornings at the Panda Base are also far quieter than the summer holidays. The base opens at **8:00 from November to February**. See [Best Time to Visit the Chengdu Panda Base](/blog/best-time-to-visit-chengdu-panda-base) for a month-by-month guide, including when the year's cubs are on show.

### Golden ginkgo (late November to mid-December)

The ginkgo is Chengdu's city tree, and for a few weeks the streets and temples turn bright yellow. Visitor guides put the best viewing from **late November to mid-December**, depending on the weather. Good places to see them include People's Park, Wenshu Monastery and Du Fu Thatched Cottage.

Fragrant yellow **wintersweet** flowers from about late December, and **plum blossoms** follow from late January into February. Du Fu Thatched Cottage and Huanhuaxi Park next to it are among the best places to see them.

### Hot pot and teahouse season

Winter is when Chengdu's food culture makes the most sense: long, steaming hot pot dinners and afternoons wrapped up with a pot of tea in the park. Ask for a split "yuanyang" pot (half spicy, half mild broth) if you're not sure about the heat. Our guide to [tea, food and neighbourhoods](/blog/tea-food-and-neighborhoods-chengdu) suggests how to plan your days around them.

### Spring Festival and lanterns

If you're in Sichuan around Chinese New Year, the region's lantern shows are spectacular. The biggest is the **Zigong Lantern Festival**, a few hours south of Chengdu, which in recent years has opened in late January and run past the Lantern Festival itself (**20 February** in 2027). Expect crowds and book transport early.

### Snow, if you want it

You won't find much snow in Chengdu, but it's not far away:

- **Xiling Snow Mountain**, about 100 km (roughly two hours by road) west of Chengdu, is the largest ski resort in southwest China. The ski season runs from about mid-December to February.
- **Mount Emei's Golden Summit** is usually snow-covered from December to February, with very cold, windy weather at the top. It pairs naturally with a visit to the Leshan Giant Buddha.

## Dates to avoid

- **Spring Festival: 5–12 February 2027.** Trains, flights and big sights are at their busiest, and some small restaurants and shops close for a few days. It's festive, but plan and book ahead.
- **New Year's Day (1 January)** is busier than usual, though nothing like Spring Festival.

Outside those dates, November, December and January are some of the quietest months to visit.

## What to pack

- A warm, water-resistant jacket (a light down jacket is ideal).
- Thermal base layers and a sweater or fleece you can wear indoors.
- Warm socks and comfortable waterproof shoes for damp days.
- A scarf and gloves for January.
- For Mount Emei or Xiling Snow Mountain: proper winter clothing, a hat and sunglasses.

## Quick checklist

- [ ] Avoiding 5–12 February 2027 (or booking early if you're coming for Spring Festival).
- [ ] Panda Base morning session booked with your passport (opens 8:00 in winter).
- [ ] Layers you can keep on indoors.
- [ ] Late November or early December if you want to see the ginkgo.
