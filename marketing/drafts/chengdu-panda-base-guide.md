## What the Panda Base is

The Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding — almost everyone just calls it the Panda Base — is a breeding and research centre in the north of Chengdu that is also open to visitors. It was founded in 1987 with six giant pandas rescued from the wild, and has since grown into one of the largest captive-breeding centres in the world, home to well over a hundred giant pandas today.

It doesn't feel like a city zoo. The grounds are large, green and hilly, with bamboo groves, lakes and spacious outdoor enclosures, and you can easily spend a whole morning walking between them. For many visitors it is the highlight of a trip to Chengdu.

## What you'll see

![Three giant pandas eating bamboo together at the Chengdu Panda Base](/images/panda-base-bamboo.webp)

*Giant pandas spend much of the morning eating bamboo. Photo: [Enricoroberto5](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Panda_giganti_al_Giant_Panda_Breeding_Research_Base_Chengdu.jpg), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0), via Wikimedia Commons.*

- **Adult giant pandas** in outdoor enclosures and "villas", eating bamboo, climbing and — very often — sleeping.
- **Subadult pandas**, the young pandas that are usually the most playful to watch.
- **Nursery houses** (such as the Sun and Star nurseries), where the base's youngest pandas are shown in season. Whether newborn cubs are on display depends on the time of year.
- **Red pandas**, which are smaller, fox-like and far more active than giant pandas. Their area is a favourite with children.
- **Panda Creek Valley** and other landscaped areas, plus exhibition spaces about panda biology and conservation.

![Three red pandas play-fighting on the grass at the Chengdu Panda Base](/images/panda-base-red-pandas.webp)

*Red pandas are much livelier than giant pandas. Photo: [MSK](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:3_red_pandas_play-fighting.jpg), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0), via Wikimedia Commons.*

Signs along the main routes are in Chinese and English, and the paths are easy to follow.

## The best time to go

Go in the **morning**, as early as you can. Pandas are most active around feeding time, roughly from opening until about 10:00. By late morning many have eaten and fallen asleep, often high up in a tree, and in summer the afternoon heat makes them even less active.

![A giant panda asleep on a wooden platform at the Chengdu Panda Base](/images/panda-base-sleeping.webp)

*By late morning, many pandas are asleep. Photo: [Colin W](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Panda_at_the_Chengdu_Research_Base_of_Giant_Panda_Breeding,_Chengdu_-_panoramio.jpg), [CC BY-SA 3.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0), via Wikimedia Commons.*

Weekdays are noticeably calmer than weekends. Chinese public holidays — especially the October Golden Week and the Spring Festival — are the busiest days of the year.

Plan on **three to four hours** for an unhurried visit.

## Tickets and booking with a passport

The base now works on advance, real-name booking. In 2026:

- **Book in advance.** Use the base's official WeChat mini program or official account (成都大熊猫繁育研究基地), or an authorised platform. Trip.com is the one most often recommended for visitors without a Chinese phone number. Bookings open up to 14 days ahead; one to three days is usually enough on weekdays, but book one to two weeks ahead for weekends and holidays.
- **Choose "passport" as your ID type.** Your ticket is tied to your passport number.
- **Bring the original passport.** It is checked at the entrance, and a photo or photocopy may not be accepted.
- **Price.** Several 2026 sources list adult admission at about **CNY 55**, with discounts for some students, children and seniors. Confirm the current price when you book.
- **Don't count on buying at the gate.** Some sources say tickets are only sold online; others say a window sometimes sells leftover tickets. Booking ahead avoids the problem.

Tickets are sold for **morning** or **afternoon** sessions, and you can't enter before your session starts. Recent booking guides list these times (they can change by season, so check the time on your booking):

| Season | Morning entry | Afternoon entry | Closes |
|---|---|---|---|
| March–October | 7:30–12:00 | 12:00–17:00 | 18:00 |
| November–February | 8:00–12:00 | 12:00–16:30 | 17:30 |

Book the morning session and aim to arrive shortly before it opens.

## Getting there

The base is in Chenghua District, in the north of the city. Keep the address in Chinese on your phone to show a driver:

**成都大熊猫繁育研究基地，成都市成华区熊猫大道1375号**
(Chengdu Research Base of Giant Panda Breeding, 1375 Panda Avenue, Chenghua District)

- **Taxi or ride-hailing (DiDi):** the easiest option for an early start, usually 30–45 minutes from the city centre depending on traffic.
- **Metro and shuttle:** Metro Line 3 serves the area, with a panda shuttle bus from the metro. Station names differ between sources, so check the current route in your map app before you set off.

There are two main visitor entrances, the **South Gate** and the **West Gate**. The South Gate is the usual choice for a first visit; the West Gate is often described as a quieter start. Either works, as long as your driver knows which one you want.

## Getting around inside

The base is big and hilly, so wear comfortable shoes. An official **sightseeing bus** loops between the South and West Gates, stopping at the main panda areas. The base lists:

- **CNY 30 per person** for visitors over 1.3 m; children 6 and under, or under 1.3 m, ride free.
- Running **7:30–17:30** (March–October) and **8:00–17:00** (November–February).
- Tickets through the official mini program or account, or on site on the day.

A good pattern is to walk to the nearest enclosures first while the pandas are active, then take the bus to cross the park.

## A few practical tips

- **Bring water and a light snack**, especially in summer. There are shops inside, but they can be far apart.
- **Be quiet and don't use flash.** The pandas are easily disturbed, and staff will remind you.
- **Rain isn't a problem.** Pandas don't mind it, and a light drizzle often thins the crowds. Bring an umbrella or raincoat.
- **Finish by lunchtime.** After a morning with the pandas, the rest of the day suits something slower — a teahouse in People's Park, for example. [Your First 48 Hours in Chengdu](/blog/first-48-hours-in-chengdu) shows how to fit the base into a relaxed first day.
