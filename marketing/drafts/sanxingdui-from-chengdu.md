## What Sanxingdui is

Sanxingdui (三星堆, "Three Star Mounds") is an archaeological site in Guanghan, just north of Chengdu, and the museum built beside it is one of the most remarkable in China. In 1986, farmers and archaeologists uncovered two pits filled with more than a thousand objects — huge bronze masks with protruding eyes, a life-size bronze figure, bronze "sacred trees", gold foil, jade and elephant tusks — left by a Bronze Age culture that flourished here roughly 3,000 years ago.

What makes Sanxingdui so striking is how *different* it looks. These faces don't resemble anything else from ancient China, and the culture left no writing that has been deciphered, so much of what the objects meant is still debated. Between 2020 and 2023, six more pits were excavated, adding gold masks, new bronzes and ivory carvings, and in July 2023 a large new museum building opened to show them.

If you have an extra day in Chengdu, this is one of the best ways to spend it.

## What you'll see

The museum has two connected buildings, and most visitors walk through both:

- **The new Comprehensive Museum** tells the story chronologically, from the site's discovery to the newest finds from the 2020–2023 excavations. Plan on two hours or more.
- **The Bronze Museum** focuses on the most famous ritual bronzes. Allow about an hour.

Highlights to look out for:

- **The Bronze Standing Figure** — about 2.6 metres tall including its base, often described as the oldest life-size bronze human figure in the world.
- **The masks with protruding eyes** — the largest is well over a metre wide, with cylindrical eyes jutting out from the face. They're the image most people associate with Sanxingdui.
- **Bronze Sacred Tree No. 1** — nearly four metres tall, with birds perched on its branches.
- **The gold masks**, including the half-mask from Pit 5 found in the recent excavations, the largest Shang-era gold mask yet discovered in China.

Signs are in Chinese and English, but the labels are short. Because there are no written records from this culture, the objects need interpreting more than most museum pieces — reading up beforehand, or going with someone who can explain them, makes a big difference.

## Tickets and booking with a passport

- **Price:** 72 RMB for general admission.
- **Book in advance.** Tickets are sold by date and time slot (morning and afternoon, plus evening slots on some dates), and weekends, public holidays and school breaks sell out. Book a few days ahead; for holidays, book as early as the system allows.
- **Where to book:** the museum's official website (sxd.cn) or its official WeChat mini program ("三星堆博物馆"), which accept passports according to a September 2026 test of the booking process. If the official channels give you trouble, Trip.com and Klook also sell tickets to foreign visitors.
- **Bring your original passport.** It's scanned at the entrance, and a photo or copy may not be accepted.

Don't plan on buying a ticket at the gate.

## Opening hours

- **08:30–18:00**, with the last entry at **17:00**.
- On some dates (public holidays and in January, February, July and August) the museum may stay open until 20:00.
- It closes only on Chinese New Year's Eve.

Hours and slots can change, so check the time on your booking.

## Getting there from Chengdu

Sanxingdui is in Guanghan, about 40 km north of central Chengdu.

- **High-speed train + bus or taxi (best):** trains from Chengdu East (some also from other Chengdu stations) reach **Guanghan North** in about 20 minutes. From the station, **bus No. 13** runs to the museum, or a taxi covers the 7 km in about 15 minutes for roughly 20 RMB. In busy periods the taxi queue at Guanghan North can be long, so the bus is often quicker.
- **Taxi or DiDi from Chengdu:** about an hour each way, roughly 150–200 RMB. Easiest with a group or children.
- **Tourist buses:** direct buses from Chengdu take 70–90 minutes; schedules vary.

Keep the name in Chinese on your phone: **三星堆博物馆，四川省广汉市** (Sanxingdui Museum, Guanghan, Sichuan).

## How long to spend, and when to go

Plan on **three to four hours** inside, plus travel. A comfortable day looks like this: an early train, arriving for the 08:30 opening; the main halls before the late-morning crowds (busiest roughly 10:30–14:30); lunch in Guanghan or near the museum; and back in Chengdu by mid-afternoon.

Weekdays are noticeably quieter than weekends.

## A few tips

- **Read a little first.** Even ten minutes on the 1986 and 2020–2023 discoveries makes the masks far more interesting.
- **Start with the new building** for the story, then finish in the Bronze Museum.
- **Wear comfortable shoes.** The two buildings are large and there's a lot of walking.
- **Pair it with something calm.** After a morning of bronzes, an evening teahouse back in Chengdu is a good balance.
