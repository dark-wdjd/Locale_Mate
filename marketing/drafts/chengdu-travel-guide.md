## Why Chengdu

Chengdu, the capital of Sichuan province, is best known for giant pandas and fiery Sichuan food, but what most visitors remember is its pace. It's a huge, modern city that still makes time for tea in the park, long lunches and games of mahjong. It's also the gateway to some of western China's most spectacular sights: the Leshan Giant Buddha, the Bronze Age masks of Sanxingdui, the ancient irrigation system at Dujiangyan, and the lakes of Jiuzhaigou.

This page is our starting point for planning a trip. Each section gives you the essentials and links to a detailed guide.

## When to go

Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are the most comfortable times to visit, with mild temperatures and fewer very hot or wet days. October and November tend to be the driest months. Summer is hot and humid, and winter is cool and damp, but neither is a reason to stay away: pandas are often more active on cool mornings.

Avoid Chinese public holidays if you can, especially the October Golden Week and the Spring Festival. Sights, trains and hotels are at their busiest then, and popular attractions sell out.

## How many days do you need?

- **2 days:** the Panda Base, a park teahouse, the old lanes and a hot pot dinner. Our [First 48 Hours in Chengdu](/blog/first-48-hours-in-chengdu) shows how to pace it.
- **3–4 days:** add one or two day trips: Sanxingdui, Leshan or Dujiangyan.
- **5 days or more:** add a slower day in the city, a cooking class or teahouse afternoon, and consider an overnight trip to Jiuzhaigou or western Sichuan.

## Getting there

Chengdu has **two airports**:

- **Tianfu International (TFU)**, the newer and larger one, about 50 km south-east of the centre. Metro Line 18 runs to Chengdu South Railway Station in about 35 minutes; change to Line 1 for the centre.
- **Shuangliu International (CTU)**, closer to the city (about 17 km). Metro Line 10 connects it to the network.

Check which airport your flight uses before you book a hotel or a transfer. Chengdu is also well connected by high-speed rail to Chongqing, Xi'an and other cities.

**Visas:** citizens of many countries can use China's **240-hour visa-free transit** policy through Chengdu's airports, staying up to 10 days in Chengdu and ten other Sichuan cities (including Leshan and Deyang, where Sanxingdui is) if they're travelling on to a third country with a confirmed onward ticket. The list of eligible countries changes, so check the official rules for your nationality before you travel.

## What to see in Chengdu

- **The Chengdu Panda Base.** The visit most people plan their trip around. Book ahead with your passport and go in the morning, when the pandas are most active. Full guide: [Chengdu Panda Base: A Simple Visitor's Guide](/blog/chengdu-panda-base-guide).
- **People's Park and its teahouses.** The best place to see everyday Chengdu: tea, card games, dancing and the famous matchmaking corner.
- **The old lanes.** Kuanzhai Alley and Jinli are busy and touristy but fun, especially in the evening. Wenshu Monastery is a calmer, working Buddhist temple nearby.
- **Du Fu Thatched Cottage and Wuhou Shrine.** Two of the city's best historic sites, for Tang poetry and Three Kingdoms history.

## Food, tea and evenings

Sichuan food is more than chilli. Expect numbing Sichuan pepper, sour and sweet flavours, and plenty of dishes that aren't spicy at all. Try hot pot at least once, and ask for a milder broth if you're unsure.

Tea culture is a big part of daily life here. Our guide to [tea, food and neighbourhoods](/blog/tea-food-and-neighborhoods-chengdu) explains how to choose experiences by interest. For evenings, see [After sunset fun in Chengdu](/blog/after-sunset-fun-in-chengdu).

## Day trips from Chengdu

- **Sanxingdui.** Extraordinary Bronze Age masks and figures, 20 minutes away by high-speed train. Full guide: [Visiting Sanxingdui from Chengdu](/blog/sanxingdui-from-chengdu).
- **Leshan Giant Buddha.** The world's largest ancient stone Buddha, carved into a cliff above the river. It's a classic day trip; many visitors combine it with Mount Emei over two days.
- **Dujiangyan and Qingcheng Mountain.** A 2,000-year-old irrigation system that still works, paired with one of the birthplaces of Taoism.
- **Jiuzhaigou and Huanglong.** Turquoise lakes and travertine terraces in the mountains north of Chengdu. Too far for a day trip; plan at least two nights.

## Getting around and paying

- **Metro:** Chengdu's metro is extensive, cheap and signposted in English. It's the easiest way to cross the city.
- **Taxis and ride-hailing:** DiDi is widely used. Keep your destination written in Chinese on your phone to show drivers.
- **Paying:** Alipay and WeChat Pay are used almost everywhere, and both let international visitors link a foreign Visa or Mastercard. Set them up before you arrive, and carry some cash as a backup. A small fee may apply to larger foreign-card payments.
- **Booking attractions:** many sites, including the Panda Base and Sanxingdui, require advance, real-name booking with your passport, and you'll need the original passport at the gate.
